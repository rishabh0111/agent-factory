# Positioning Options and Value Proposition Statements

Two generation branches that feed the context document. **Positioning options** run when there is no positioning yet or the user is repositioning: generate differentiated options, the user picks one, and the winner goes into the doc. **Value proposition statements** run once positioning is settled: turn it into segment-specific lines for marketing, sales and onboarding.

Start from what the doc already holds (Target Audience, Competitive Landscape, Differentiation, Customer Language). Use customer language over internal jargon, and tie every line to the customer's job to be done rather than to a feature list.

## Positioning Options

### Step 1: Map the competitive landscape
Identify the top 5 competitors (from the doc, competitor-profiling dossiers, or web research). For each, note:
- Their primary positioning angle
- The audience they focus on
- The differentiators they emphasize
- The positioning gaps they leave open

**Done when** all five have an angle and at least one open gap.

### Step 2: Generate 5 positioning options
Each option must:
- Be clearly distinct from every competitor's positioning
- Speak to the target audience's values and needs
- Lead with capabilities competitors downplay or ignore
- Claim territory no one else holds

### Step 3: Write each option out
1. **Positioning statement** in one sentence. Two forms:
   - "[Product] is the only [category] designed for [segment] who want to [primary benefit]."
   - "For [who] who [need], [product] is [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [differentiator]."
2. **Strategic rationale**: why it resonates with the audience and creates differentiation
3. **Supporting messages**: the two or three messages that reinforce it
4. **Competitive advantage**: what about the product makes the claim credible
5. **Strength and risk**: how hard it is to copy, and what could undercut it

Recommend one option and say why. Positioning is a hypothesis: suggest testing the leading options as headlines with the **ab-testing** skill before rewriting every page. Once the user picks, write the winner into the doc's One-liner and Differentiation sections, with a version bump and changelog entry.

## Value Proposition Statements

For each target segment or use case, write a statement that:
- Names the segment or use case directly
- Leads with the primary benefit and the outcome they want
- Names the capabilities that make it possible
- Uses plain language the segment would use

Produce the lengths each context needs:
- **Tagline**: one line
- **Elevator pitch**: two or three sentences
- **Landing page hero**: headline and subheading
- **Sales one-liner**: for conversations
- **Onboarding message**: what a new user in this segment hears first

### Example (Canva)

1. **For social media marketers**: Canva lets social media marketers create on-brand designs without expensive design software or a dedicated designer, so they produce professional graphics that lift engagement and keep the brand consistent across channels.
2. **For small business owners**: With drag-and-drop editing and ready-made templates, small business owners launch polished campaigns in minutes: website graphics, social posts, flyers and promotions that look professionally designed, with no design experience.
3. **For content creators**: Canva lets creators spend their time on storytelling instead of design logistics, producing consistent visuals at scale with templates sized for each platform.
