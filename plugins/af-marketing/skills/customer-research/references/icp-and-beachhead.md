# ICP Definition and Beachhead Segment

Two research-to-decision deliverables. The **ideal customer profile (ICP)** says who gets the most value, retains and expands, drawn from customers you already have or have researched. The **beachhead segment** picks the single first market to dominate before expanding, and suits a new product or a new market where few customers exist yet. Run the ICP when there is customer data to mine; run the beachhead when the question is "which segment first?"

Both rest on the extraction framework and confidence guardrails in the main skill: at least 5 independent data points per segment (aim for 10 interviews), every claim labelled High, Medium or Low confidence.

## ICP Definition

Grounded in Jobs to Be Done (Clayton Christensen): combine behavioural data with motivation.

### Step 1: Gather customer data
PMF survey responses (start from the "very disappointed" group when a survey exists), interview transcripts, trial or freemium behaviour, support tickets and feedback, churn and lifecycle data, win/loss notes, and what competitors' customers say.

### Step 2: Segment by value
Find the customers with the highest LTV, fastest time-to-value, lowest churn, most expansion, most engagement, best case-study potential and closest fit with the product vision. The ICP is drawn from where these overlap. **Done when** the high-value group is named, with its size and the data that defines it.

### Step 3: Profile the high-value group on four dimensions

**Firmographics and demographics**: company size (employees, revenue), industry and sub-vertical, geography, company stage, department and reporting line, budget holder and available budget, job title, years in role, background, culture signals.

**Behaviours**: how they discover and evaluate solutions (and which channel found you), buying process and timeline, stakeholders in the decision (solo or committee), obstacles in the sales process, adoption speed and breadth, team involvement in onboarding, feature usage frequency, support needs, technical literacy, tool-switching habits, community involvement and peer influence.

**Jobs to be done**: the primary functional job, supporting jobs, emotional jobs (how they want to feel), social jobs (how they want to be seen), jobs they want eliminated, the context (when, where, with whom), how they measure success, and how each job ranks in frequency and importance.

**Needs and pain points**: the before state and its frustrations, the desired after state, gap size and quantified impact (time, cost, outcomes), emotional weight, current workarounds and their limits, constraints blocking a fix, skepticism or hesitations, competing priorities, and their success criteria for a solution.

### Step 4: Draw the boundary
Write disqualification criteria (who is not a fit, and why), the acceptable secondary segments, and the ideal-of-the-ideal inside the ICP. The ICP drives focus; it doesn't forbid selling to everyone else.

### ICP output
- Firmographic profile
- Behavioural profile (buying pattern, adoption style)
- JTBD map: functional, emotional, social
- Top 5–7 pain points and needs
- Quantified impact: cost of the problem, value of the solution
- Decision process and key stakeholders
- Typical customer journey and timeline
- Qualification signals that let sales or marketing spot a fit in 30 seconds
- Disqualification criteria
- Ideal-of-the-ideal segment
- Go-to-market implications and messaging

Offer to write the ICP into the Target Audience section of `.agents/product-marketing.md` through the **product-marketing** skill, and suggest revisiting it quarterly as data grows.

## Beachhead Segment

Based on Geoffrey Moore's beachhead strategy in *Crossing the Chasm*: find the smallest winnable, referenceable market that validates product-market fit and opens adjacent segments.

### The four criteria

1. **Burning pain**: an acute, unmet problem. Daily frustration with the status quo, real productivity or cost impact, emotional urgency, expensive or fragile workarounds, a problem that is getting worse.
2. **Willingness to pay**: budget and motivation. Budget already allocated to the problem area, clear ROI, a decision-maker with budget authority, and no free or DIY alternative that fully meets the need.
3. **Winnable share**: can you realistically take 60–70% of the segment in 3–18 months? Large enough but not saturated, fragmented or complacent incumbents, a clear advantage, and unique access or distribution.
4. **Referral potential**: professional communities inside the segment, strong word-of-mouth culture, network effects, and adjacent segments that look to this one for references.

### Steps

1. **List candidate segments** across industry verticals, company size, roles, geographies, use cases and customer maturity. Start absurdly specific: a niche beats a vague mass market.
2. **Validate pain** per segment from interviews, surveys, analyst reports, competitor reviews and positioning; quantify the cost of the problem and map current workarounds.
3. **Assess willingness to pay**: category budget, ROI, current spend on workarounds, the budget process, typical deal size and price sensitivity.
4. **Evaluate winnability**: TAM for the segment (sizing method in the marketing-plan market-sizing reference), competitive landscape, your differentiation or unfair advantage, distribution access, time and resources needed, market momentum.
5. **Map referral pathways**: which adjacent segments this one influences, communities and associations, and the natural expansion path.
6. **Score and select**: score each segment 1–5 on the four criteria, then pick the one with the highest combined score that you can win with current resources, has the shortest path to revenue and references, and has the most enthusiastic early adopters. **Done when** one segment is chosen and each criterion score cites its evidence and confidence level.

Put every resource into dominating the beachhead; move to the next segment only after reaching roughly 60% share.

### Beachhead output
- Top 3–5 segments with scores and evidence
- The recommended beachhead and why
- Pain validation and willingness-to-pay assessment, with pricing guidance
- Realistic share and revenue projections, with assumptions
- Referral and expansion pathways to adjacent segments
- A 90-day customer acquisition plan for the beachhead
- The post-beachhead expansion roadmap
