# PESTLE Analysis

Scan the external forces that could shape product strategy, market entry or business viability, and turn each material one into a response.

Read `company.md` first for the product, business type and markets; ask only for what is missing: the geography, the strategic question behind the scan, and any known regulatory or market changes. Use web research for current regulations, market data and trend signals; cite each source next to the factor.

## Factors to consider

- **Political**: government policy and incentives, tax and tariffs, political stability, public spending and subsidies, trade agreements, licensing and permits, lobbying needs.
- **Economic**: growth and GDP trends, interest rates and inflation, exchange rates, consumer spending and confidence, labour costs, disposable income, access to capital.
- **Social**: demographics, cultural attitudes and values, lifestyles and behaviours, skills availability, health trends, digital adoption, diversity and inclusion expectations.
- **Technological**: emerging technologies (AI, cloud and others), digital transformation, cybersecurity and privacy requirements, automation, IoT, R&D capability, adoption rates and digital literacy.
- **Legal**: data protection (GDPR, CCPA and local equivalents), employment law, IP and patents, consumer protection, industry-specific regulation, compliance and audit costs, liability and insurance.
- **Environmental**: climate regulation, emissions and sustainability requirements, resource scarcity, waste and circular economy, renewable energy, ESG expectations, green certifications.

Some factors span categories (a regulation can be political, legal and economic at once): place it where its impact lands and note the others.

## Steps

1. **Identify.** List 3-5 specific factors per category for this market and geography: named regulations, figures and trends rather than generic observations. Done when all six categories have factors or an explicit "not material, because...".
2. **Rate.** Give each factor impact (High / Medium / Low), likelihood (High / Medium / Low), trend and timeframe, then rank by impact × likelihood. Done when every factor has all four.
3. **Respond.** For each high-ranked factor, say whether it is an opportunity to use, a threat to mitigate, or a compliance need to plan for, and the response. Build contingency plans for the high-impact threats. Done when every high-ranked factor has a response.
4. **Monitor.** Name the leading indicator, source and check frequency for each high-ranked factor, and list the assumptions and unknowns that need research. Done when the monitoring table covers every high-ranked factor.

PESTLE is the macro view; pair it with `swot-analysis` for the company's internal position and `porters-five-forces` for industry structure. Reassess yearly or when markets shift.
