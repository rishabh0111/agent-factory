# Customer Journey Map

Map the end-to-end customer experience, stage by stage, with the emotions, pain points and improvement opportunities at each. Emotions matter as much as actions: where users feel frustrated or delighted drives prioritization.

Read `company.md` first for the product and customers; ask only for what is missing. Read any interview transcripts, survey data, analytics, support tickets or existing journey maps the user provides. When the user gives a product URL, research the product on the web. With no research data, label the map as hypotheses and name how to validate each stage.

## Steps

1. **Persona.** Name who travels this journey: one specific persona with their job to be done, not a generic user. The `user-personas` skill builds one from research. Done when the persona and job fit in two sentences.
2. **Stages.** Adapt these to the product:

   | Stage | Question |
   |---|---|
   | Awareness | How do they first learn about the product? |
   | Consideration | What do they evaluate? Which alternatives do they compare? |
   | Acquisition | How do they sign up or buy? |
   | Onboarding | First experience: how long until value? |
   | Engagement | Regular use: is a habit forming? |
   | Retention | What keeps them coming back? What might make them churn? |
   | Expansion | When do they grow their usage, seats or plan? (include when the product has upgrades) |
   | Advocacy | When and why do they recommend the product? |

   Done when every stage applies to this product or is dropped with a reason.
3. **Per stage**, record: touchpoints (website, email, in-app, support, social), user actions, thoughts and questions ("Is this worth my time?"), emotion (a -2 to +2 rating), pain points (friction, confusion, drop-off risk), and opportunities. Done when every stage has every field.
4. **Critical moments.** Mark the **aha moment** (first experience of core value), the **moments of truth** (decision points where they commit or abandon) and the **churn triggers** (where users most often drop off), plus moments of delight worth amplifying. Done when each is tied to a stage and to evidence or a stated hypothesis.
5. **Prioritized improvements.** Rank pain points by their effect on conversion or retention, then split into quick wins and larger investments with the biggest payoff. Done when the top 3 improvements each name the stage, pain point and expected effect.

## Output

| Stage | Touchpoints | User action | Thoughts | Emotion | Pain point | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then the critical moments and the prioritized improvements. Save as markdown; for a visual map, suggest building it in a whiteboard tool from this table.
