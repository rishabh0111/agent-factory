# Porter's Five Forces

Rate the five structural forces of an industry, find the two or three that decide profitability for this company, and plan a response to each.

Read `company.md` first for the product, customers and competitors; ask only for what is missing: the industry definition, the supplier and customer landscape, and likely substitutes and entrants. Use web research to name specific players and dynamics.

## The forces

Each force is **high** when most of its "high" signals hold. Rate each one High / Medium / Low.

| Force | High when | Low when | Typical responses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Competitive rivalry** | Many similar-sized rivals; slow growth (zero-sum); low differentiation; high fixed costs; high exit barriers; price wars; rivals with diverse goals or emotional commitment | Few rivals; fast growth; strong differentiation; low fixed costs; a clear leader; compatible goals | Sharpen differentiation or cost leadership; define defensible advantages; watch competitor moves and consolidation |
| **Supplier power** | Few or concentrated suppliers; high switching costs; suppliers could integrate forward; critical or unique inputs; no substitutes; suppliers not dependent on you | Many suppliers; low switching costs; suppliers depend on you; commodity inputs; you could integrate backward; substitutes exist | Diversify suppliers; strengthen relationships; consider vertical integration; negotiate long-term terms; invest in partners' success |
| **Buyer power** | Few large customers; easy, frequent switching; buyers could build it themselves; undifferentiated product; tight budgets; full information on alternatives; buyers can bypass you | Many fragmented customers; high switching costs (lock-in, integration, training); strong differentiation; dependence on your product; strong brand; switching is risky | Build loyalty and customer success; create switching costs through integration; invest in brand; build network effects or community; segment by willingness to pay |
| **Threat of substitutes** | Good, accessible alternatives; similar or better value; low switching costs; customers willing to try; substitutes improving faster; disruptive technology | No good substitutes; substitutes costlier or worse; deep workflow integration; strong loyalty; the product solves the problem uniquely | Monitor emerging substitutes; deepen integration and stickiness; keep innovating; build an ecosystem; defend through brand, service or convenience |
| **Threat of new entrants** | Low capital, expertise or licensing barriers; attractive margins and growth; complacent incumbents; open distribution; weak scale economies or network effects; permissive regulation; enabling new technology | High barriers (capital, IP, relationships); entrenched scale; strong network effects; brand loyalty; regulatory barriers; control of key resources or distribution; credible retaliation | Build barriers (IP, brand, network effects); pursue scale and cost advantage; create switching costs; watch startups; form alliances and control key resources |

**Industry attractiveness**: attractive when rivalry is low, supplier and buyer power weak, substitutes few and entry barriers high; unattractive in the reverse; moderate when mixed, which calls for strategic differentiation. Position matters: the same industry can be attractive for one company and not another.

## Steps

1. **Define the industry** and the company's position in it. Done when the boundary (who is in, who is out) is one sentence.
2. **Rate each force**: intensity, current state and trend (strengthening or weakening), the specific players and drivers, and the implication for profit. Done when every force names concrete drivers, not just a rating.
3. **Judge attractiveness** overall and for this company specifically. Done when the rating has a stated reason.
4. **Respond.** Pick the 2-3 forces most critical to the strategy; for each, how to reduce a strong force or exploit a weak one, and the positioning opportunity it opens. Done when each critical force has a strategic initiative.

Use with `swot-analysis` and `pestle-analysis` for a full picture. Forces shift over time; reassess as the market changes.
