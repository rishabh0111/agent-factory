# Market Sizing (TAM, SAM, SOM)

Estimate the Total Addressable Market, Serviceable Addressable Market and Serviceable Obtainable Market, top-down and bottom-up, with growth projections and the assumptions behind every number.

Read `company.md` first for the product, customers, pricing and geography; ask only for what is missing. Read any market research, industry reports, financial or competitor data the user provides, and search the web for current industry data, analyst reports and benchmarks.

## Rules

- **Triangulate.** Every TAM has a top-down and a bottom-up estimate, reconciled.
- **Cite every market number**, and label each figure as *data* or *estimate*.
- **State the unit.** Say whether sizing is by value (annual revenue) or volume (users, units), and keep it consistent.
- **Ranges over false precision.** Flag wide confidence intervals.
- For international markets, note currency and purchasing-power differences.

## Steps

1. **Define the market**: problem space and customer need, customer segments, geography, and other scoping decisions. Done when someone else could reproduce the boundary.
2. **Top-down**: start from total industry size and narrow to the relevant slice, citing each source.
3. **Bottom-up**: customers × price × purchase frequency, from unit economics.
4. **Reconcile** the two; explain the gap and pick the TAM figure. Done when both estimates and the reasoning for the chosen figure are shown.
5. **SAM**: the share of TAM the product can realistically serve given geography, language, channels, product capabilities and pricing tier. Give it as a percentage of TAM with reasoning.
6. **SOM**: the share achievable in 1-3 years given competitive position, go-to-market capacity and current traction. Give it as a percentage of SAM with reasoning.
7. **Growth**: project TAM, SAM and SOM over 2-3 years; name the drivers (technology, regulatory, demographic, behavioral shifts, emerging segments, adjacent markets) that could expand or contract them.
8. **Assumptions**: number every critical assumption, rate confidence (high / medium / low), say how to validate the shakiest, what would materially change the estimate, and which data sources or research would sharpen it. Done when every number in the summary traces to a source or a numbered assumption.

## Output

**Market definition**, then TAM, SAM and SOM sections with their reasoning, then:

| Metric | Current estimate | 2-3 year projection |
|---|---|---|
| TAM | | |
| SAM | | |
| SOM | | |

Then **Growth drivers and trends** and **Key assumptions and risks**. Save as markdown.
