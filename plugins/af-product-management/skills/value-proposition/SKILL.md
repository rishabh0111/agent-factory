# Value Proposition

Write a value proposition per customer segment with the 6-part jobs-to-be-done template: Who, Why, What before, How, What after, Alternatives. It starts from the customer and works toward the solution, takes one segment per pass, and forces you to name the alternatives.

Read `company.md` first for the product, customers and positioning; ask only for what is missing: the segment, today's alternatives and workarounds, and any customer research. Read any pitch decks, PRDs or competitive analyses the user provides.

## The template

1. **Who**: the segment this is for, its characteristics and constraints.
2. **Why**: the job to be done, the desired outcome, and the problem in the way.
3. **What before**: the current situation: what they use today and the friction or pain in it.
4. **How**: the specific features or capabilities that get the job done, and why they beat the alternatives.
5. **What after**: the improved outcome: how their life or work changes, and what becomes possible that wasn't before.
6. **Alternatives**: what they would use without us, why they would choose us instead, and the switching cost or friction.

**Example (Canva).** Who: non-designers who make marketing graphics. Why: they need professional-looking designs but can't hire designers or use complex tools. What before: PowerPoint, Photoshop (too complex) or expensive designers. How: drag-and-drop templates, built-in design elements, AI assistance, an intuitive interface. What after: professional designs in minutes, faster campaigns, lower design costs. Alternatives: Photoshop (complex), Fiverr (slow, expensive), competitors with fewer templates and harder UX.

## Steps

1. **One segment at a time.** Fill the six parts for the first segment; repeat for each further segment. Done when every part is specific to that segment.
2. **Statement.** Write a one- or two-sentence statement: "For [who] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [differentiator]." Done when it names the alternative.
3. **Reusable versions.** Adapt the statement for marketing, sales and onboarding. Done when all three exist.
4. **Compare.** When competitors matter to the decision, add a Value Curve (Blue Ocean Strategy): plot our offering and the alternatives across the key factors customers weigh.

Value propositions are segment-specific; test them with real customers before treating them as settled. Choose Strategyzer's Value Proposition Canvas instead only when a mature product needs a detailed decomposition of customer jobs, pains and gains.
