# Startup Canvas

Part 1 is the product strategy (the nine sections of the Product Strategy Canvas; the `product-strategy` skill holds the full guidance). Part 2 is the business model. Keep them distinct but connected.

## Part 1: Product strategy

1. **Vision**: How do we inspire people? What are we aspiring to achieve? What values do we uphold? Start simple; the vision evolves with the strategy.
2. **Market segments**: the market is defined by people's problems, not demographics: jobs to be done, desired outcomes, constraints. Which segment comes first, and why?
3. **Relative costs**: optimize for low cost (like Southwest Airlines) or unique value (like Starbucks)? Low costs don't necessarily mean low prices.
4. **Value proposition**, per segment: what before (the problematic state), how (features and capabilities that change it), what after (benefits and outcomes), alternatives (our unique value vs competitors and substitutes; consider a Value Curve).
5. **Trade-offs**: what we will NOT do. Especially important for startups, where it is tempting to chase every opportunity.
6. **Key metrics**: a few metrics that show whether product and strategy work: the North Star Metric and this quarter's One Metric That Matters.
7. **Growth**: product-led or sales-led? Preferred channels: social, SEO, influencers, resellers?
8. **Capabilities**: competencies and resources to acquire; build or partner?
9. **Can't / Won't**: why competitors can't or won't copy the strategy. The whole strategy should be hard to copy, not one element. Do all the elements fit together and reinforce each other?

## Part 2: Business model

10. **Cost structure**: rent, hardware, licences, technology, marketing, subscriptions, salaries. Which recur, and how do they scale?
11. **Revenue streams**: how much from each channel? Pricing approach: penetration, value-based, competitive, usage-based, subscription? Is the model scalable, and what are the biggest uncertainties?
