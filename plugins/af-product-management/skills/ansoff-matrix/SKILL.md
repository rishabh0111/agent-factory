# Ansoff Matrix

Map growth options on two axes, products and markets, and pick the quadrant with the best risk-reward for this company now.

Read `company.md` first for the products, customers, markets and goals; ask only for what is missing: current penetration and performance, growth targets and timeline, capabilities and constraints, and the competitive dynamics.

## The matrix

|  | Current market | New market |
|---|---|---|
| **Current product** | Market penetration | Market development |
| **New product** | Product development | Diversification |

| Quadrant | Grow by | Typical moves | Examples | Risk | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Market penetration** | More usage or sales in the existing market | Raise usage frequency, expand use cases, win competitors' customers, cut churn, upsell and cross-sell, lower prices for price-sensitive segments, raise awareness, improve experience to drive referrals | Netflix adding games for engagement; Starbucks encouraging repeat weekly visits | Low | 6-12 months |
| **Market development** | Selling the existing product to new segments or geographies | New regions, new personas, new channels or partners, adapting for new use cases, localization | Uber entering new cities; Slack selling beyond tech | Medium | 12-24 months |
| **Product development** | New products or features for existing customers | Adjacent product lines, bundles, premium or lite versions, complementary products, integrated adjacent capabilities | Spotify adding podcasts; Figma adding FigJam | Medium | 12-18 months |
| **Diversification** | New products in new markets | Related diversification (existing competencies) or unrelated, acquisitions, joint ventures, new business units | Amazon launching AWS; Apple moving into phones and services | High | 24+ months, heavy investment |

## Steps

1. **Baseline.** State the current product(s) and market precisely. Done when both fit in one sentence each.
2. **Opportunities.** For each quadrant, name 2-3 specific opportunities for this company, each with market size and growth potential, resources and investment, competitive dynamics, and a success metric. Done when every quadrant has at least two concrete options, not generic moves.
3. **Prioritize.** Score the options on strategic fit with the vision, revenue potential and growth rate, resource feasibility, competitive advantage and defensibility, and time to profitability. Answer: which quadrant has the best risk-reward, where our capabilities give an edge, which partnerships or acquisitions would speed things up, and how each option affects brand and positioning. Done when the top 2-3 options are ranked with reasons.
4. **Plan the top options.** For each of the top 2-3: go-to-market approach, phased milestones, risks with mitigations, and leading indicators. Done when every top option has all four.

## Rules of thumb

- Penetration is the lowest risk and diversification the highest.
- Excel in one quadrant before expanding; spreading across all four at once thins the team.
- A common sequence is penetration first, then market development.
- Reassess yearly or when market conditions shift.
