# Trio ideation

The branch of `product-brainstorming` for a structured idea set: 15 ideas from the three seats of the product trio, ranked to a top 5. The product trio (Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*) is the PM, designer and engineer doing discovery together; the best ideas often come from engineers. Discovery loops: if the experiments on the top ideas fail, come back here.

Read any research data, opportunity trees, personas, market research or competitive analysis the user provides. When the user names a product URL, research the product on the web.

## Pick the stage

- **Existing product** (continuous discovery, running alongside delivery on a live product): ground the ideas in current user behavior and validated problems. Ask what the product is and who uses it, the opportunity or problem space, constraints (technical debt, platform limits, team capacity), and what has been tried before.
- **New product** (initial discovery: testing whether the product should exist at all, alongside vision, business model and market validation): ask for the concept and the problem it solves, the target user and their current alternative, the stage (napkin sketch, validated problem, early prototype), and the riskiest assumptions.

## Steps

1. **Understand the opportunity.** Confirm the product, objective, target segment and desired outcomes; ask about anything ambiguous. If the user brought research, pull its insights first. Done when the opportunity fits one sentence.
2. **Ideate, 5 ideas per seat.** Breadth first: generate all 15 before evaluating any.

   | Seat | Existing product | New product |
   |---|---|---|
   | Product manager | business value, strategic alignment, customer impact | market fit, value creation, competitive advantage |
   | Product designer | user experience, usability, accessibility, delight | user experience, onboarding, engagement |
   | Software engineer | technical possibilities, data leverage, scalable solutions | technical innovation, API integrations, platform capabilities |

   Give each idea a name, a one-sentence description, its impact on the target user (H/M/L) and effort (H/M/L). Done when there are 15 distinct ideas.
3. **Rank the top 5** across all seats.
   - Existing product: strategic alignment with the objective, impact on the desired outcomes, feasibility and effort, differentiation from existing solutions.
   - New product: core value delivery (does it solve the primary problem?), speed to validate, differentiation potential.

   Done when each of the 5 has the reason it was chosen, its key assumptions to test, and a quick-win or strategic-bet label.

## Output

```
## Brainstorm: [Product / area]
Mode: ideas for [existing / new] product
Context: [1-2 sentences]

### PM perspective
1. **[Idea]**: [description] | Impact: [H/M/L] | Effort: [H/M/L]
### Designer perspective
### Engineer perspective

### Top 5
| Rank | Idea | Why | Key assumptions | Quick win? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### Next steps
```

Then return to the sparring session: take a position on the strongest idea and push on it. Save as markdown when substantial.
